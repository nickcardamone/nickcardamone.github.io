How to read this

Measure search was conducted Summer–Fall 2021. Of the scales screened, most were set aside because they were too long for routine use, could not be located in full, shipped without scoring instructions, or sat behind copyright or cost. What remains is the set you can actually pick up and use.

Construct definitions are reproduced from Table 1 of Stewart, Cardamone, Schachter, Becker, McKay & Becker-Haimes (2023), A systematic review of brief, freely accessible, and valid self-report measures for substance use disorders and treatment, Drug and Alcohol Dependence, 243, 109729.

Psychometric ratings

Ratings follow the rubric in De Los Reyes & Langer (2018). Each measure is graded Adequate, Good, or Excellent on the strength of its published evidence:

Internal consistency
The degree to which items on the measure describe the same construct.
Test–retest reliability
Scoring consistency for an unchanged respondent taking the measure twice.
Normative data
Published means and standard deviations, or cut-offs separating clinically significant scores.
Validity evidence
Concurrent, convergent, discriminant, and predictive evidence useful for clinical decisions.
Treatment sensitivity
How responsive the scale is to change brought on by treatment or recovery.

Access

Print-ready means the link opens a version you can use as-is. Needs formatting means the measure is available but may need to be laid out before use. A dashed outline marks the handful of measures that are validated and brief but not freely distributable.
