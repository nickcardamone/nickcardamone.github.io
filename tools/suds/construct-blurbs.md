# Construct definitions for /suds-measures

These one-line definitions appear on the site: in the tooltip when someone hovers
a construct blob, and under each construct heading in the list view.

**Nothing here publishes until `status:` reads `yours`.** `build_data.py` skips
every entry marked `draft`, so an unreviewed definition simply does not render.

## Source

The definitions below are Table 1 ("Substance use disorder constructs") of:

> Stewart, R.E., Cardamone, N.C., Schachter, A., Becker, C., McKay, J.R., &
> Becker-Haimes, E.M. (2023). A systematic review of brief, freely accessible,
> and valid self-report measures for substance use disorders and treatment.
> *Drug and Alcohol Dependence*, 243, 109729.
> https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2022.109729

They are marked `yours` because they are your own authored, peer-reviewed text,
which is the standard this gate exists to enforce. They replace nine definitions
Claude invented on 2026-08-19, which were briefly live and have been discarded.

Two things to check before this stays as-is:

2. The paper labels this construct "Cravings/Urges"; the dataset key is "Urges".

Format: a `##` heading naming the construct exactly as it appears in the data,
then a `status:` line, then the definition.

## Use
status: yours
Identifies, screens, or indicates clinically significant substance use problems; typically administered upon entering treatment and has a discrete outcome (e.g., cut-off scores separating those with and without clinically significant substance use disorder).

## Severity
status: yours
Measures degree of drug or alcohol use disorder, typically with a continuous scale.

## Consequences
status: yours
Measures problems, dysfunctions, and negative life outcomes (e.g., social, emotional, physical) related to substance use.

## Withdrawal
status: yours
Evaluates physical withdrawal symptoms attributed to substance use cessation.

## Urges
status: yours
Evaluates psychological withdrawal symptoms, inclinations, or feelings that may be felt in the absence of physical withdrawal symptoms.

## Expectancies
status: yours
The beliefs regarding the positive and negative behavioral, cognitive, and emotional effects of a substance (Cooper et al., 2016).

## Motives
status: yours
The use of a given substance in order to achieve a desired effect or outcome (Cooper et al., 2016).

## Self-Efficacy
status: yours
The belief that one has the ability to implement the behaviors to produce a desired effect (Kadden and Litt, 2011).

## Recovery
status: yours
Assess a patient's desire for behavior change, engagement with treatment or abstinence, as well as cognitions, attitudes, and beliefs related to recovery.
